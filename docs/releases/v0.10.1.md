## Films on the TV

- **Fixed: LANcast closing by itself partway through a film.** With frame-rate matching on, the desktop app closed after about an hour of playback. If it closed on you in 0.10.0, this is why.
- **Films on the TV are much smoother.** Frames used to be lost on their way to the screen, up to 280 in two minutes on a desk with other monitors on, each one a small hitch. Once the controls fade, LANcast now takes its page off the screen, so Windows can send the film straight to the TV. Measured on a 55" TV: no frames lost in two minutes of play. This needs:
  - **The TV as your main display**, in Windows' display settings (Settings › System › Display). With another screen as the main one, Windows still paces the film to that screen, and some stutter remains. LANcast doesn't change your main display for you; the setting and the log say when it would help.
  - **The film fullscreen.** With matching on, films now open fullscreen by themselves. Turn this off under **Theater output › Open films fullscreen**. F or Escape leaves fullscreen as usual.
  - Subtitled films still play the old way, because LANcast draws the subtitles over the picture.
- **Showing the controls costs a small hitch.** Bringing the controls back, and their fading away again, each make the film skip a frame or two while Windows changes how it draws the screen. So small bumps of the mouse don't wake them. A deliberate move over the film does, and so does any key, which also does what it says, so Space pauses straight away.
- **The controls fade after a matched start.** Before, if the TV took a few seconds to change rate, the controls stayed up until you moved the mouse.
- **HDR films can reach an HDR TV as HDR.** Turn on **Theater output › Send HDR to the display**, and turn on **Use HDR** for the TV in Windows' display settings. An HDR film is then handed to the TV as HDR instead of being converted to SDR first. Off by default. Still being tuned.

## Metadata

- **No sign-up needed for posters, descriptions or subtitles.** LANcast now comes with its own TMDB and OpenSubtitles keys. A key you entered yourself still takes priority, and **Settings › Metadata** says which one each service is using. You can turn the built-in keys off there; with them off and no key of your own, neither service is contacted.
- **OpenSubtitles login.** Optionally sign in with your OpenSubtitles account for a larger daily download allowance.
- OMDb still needs your own key; its row now links to where to get one. **Settings › About** lists the data sources LANcast uses.

## Settings and reporting

- **Search settings.** A search box at the top of Settings finds a single setting by name or by another word for it: *replaygain*, *2fa*, *nvenc*. Choosing a result opens the pane and points at the setting.
- **Pictures in Report an issue.** Add up to four pictures, or paste a screenshot straight into the form. LANcast strips their location data and other metadata before sending.
- **If LANcast crashes, it now keeps a record.** `lancast-client-crash.log` and `lancastd-crash.log` hold where it happened, so the next report can say why.

## Updating

From 0.9.79 or later, use the in-app update. From anything older, run **`LANcast-Setup-0.10.1.exe`**.

**No database change in this release**, so 0.10.0 can be reinstalled over it if you need to go back.

The fullscreen and HDR settings, and the smoother playback, are part of the desktop app. Quit LANcast from the tray and open it again after updating.
