## Films

- **Your TV can switch to each film's own frame rate.** Most films are made at 24 frames a second. Shown on a 60 Hz screen, slow camera pans judder. Turn on **Settings › This device › Player › Theater output › Match the display to the film's frame rate**, and the screen the player is on switches to the film's rate for as long as it plays: 23.976 Hz for most films, 25 for European TV, 29.97 for American. This is the desktop app's player only, and it's off by default.
  - **The film waits for the screen.** A TV goes black for a moment while it changes rate, so the film starts afterwards rather than losing its opening to the black screen. 3 seconds by default; change it under **Wait for the screen** if the first moment is still missing.
  - **Only that screen changes**, never your main display, and only to a rate it offers at its current resolution. A screen with no matching rate is left alone.
  - **The screen goes back by itself**: a few seconds after the film stops (the next film in a queue keeps it, so a queue doesn't make the TV blink between films), when you quit LANcast, and, if LANcast closes unexpectedly, the next time it opens. If you add, remove or move a monitor while a film plays, the TV goes back straight away.
- **Smoothest with the TV as your only display.** With other monitors on at different rates, Windows paces the picture to one of them. LANcast notices, and keeps a timing that stays smooth on a busy desk.

## Updating

From 0.9.79 or later, use the in-app update. From anything older, run **`LANcast-Setup-0.10.0.exe`**.

**No database change in this release**, so 0.9.99 can be reinstalled over it if you need to go back.

The new setting is part of the desktop app. If it doesn't appear after updating, quit LANcast from the tray and open it again.
