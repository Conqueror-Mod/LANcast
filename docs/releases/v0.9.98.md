## Comics

- **A new library type, Comics**, for `.cbz` and `.pdf` comics. Each issue's cover is its first page, the series is the folder it's in, and the title and year come from the filename.
- **Read on the comic's page**, one page at a time, fitted to the screen. Turn pages with the arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Space, a tap on either side, or a swipe. Home and End jump to the ends, and Escape goes back.
- **Your place is kept.** The page shows *Continue reading · page 5 of 12*, and reaching the last page marks the issue read.
- **PDF comics are read page by page as the pictures they are**, so they look exactly as published. A PDF page that's text rather than a picture says so in its place.
- **`.cbr` and `.cb7` aren't read yet.** A scan now tells you how many it skipped instead of showing an empty library.

## Watching

- **Chapters on the scrub bar.** Films with chapters show them as ticks, with the chapter's name when you hover. Page Up and Page Down jump between them.
- **A picture over the scrub bar.** Hover or drag along the bar to see the moment you'd land on. Turn it on in **Playback & progress › Show a picture over the scrub bar**. It's off by default because it reads every film and episode once in the background, about 40 seconds and 2 MB each. **A film you open goes to the front of the queue**, so its pictures appear within a minute or so.
- **Extras on the film's page.** Trailers, featurettes and other extras in a film's or show's folder show as a row on its page. A local trailer replaces the online one.
- **Pre-roll.** Point **Playback & progress › Pre-roll** at a folder of short clips, such as a cinema intro, and one plays at random before a film started from the beginning. Never on a resume, never before an episode. Anyone can skip it or turn it off for themselves. The setting says how many clips the folder holds, and warns if there are none.
- **The docked mini player has rounded corners**, and its border is even on every side.

## Friends

- **Invite a friend with a link.** **Users & access › Invite a friend** makes a link that works once, for 7 days. Whoever opens it chooses their own name and password and joins as a member. You can set their rating limit and which libraries they can't see before they arrive. Nobody sends anybody a password.
- **A friend's server no longer has to be reachable from yours.** If your server can be reached, a friend can pair with it, show up in your People list, and join your film without opening anything on their router. Their server sends its list of people to yours rather than waiting to be asked. Both servers need this version. While their server can't be reached, you won't see what *they're* watching, and People says so.

## Sign-in

- **Two-factor sign-in.** Turn it on in **Your account › Security** with any authenticator app, and save the ten recovery codes it shows you. An administrator can turn it off for someone who has lost their phone, but can't turn it on for them.

## Dashboard

- **What your server has been doing.** **Status › Dashboard** now shows the last 6, 24 or 48 hours, a reading a minute: people watching, conversions, the whole computer's CPU, the server's memory, requests, and errors. It's kept through restarts. Network use isn't shown yet.

## Updating

From 0.9.79 or later, use the in-app update. From anything older, run **`LANcast-Setup-0.9.98.exe`**.

**This update changes the database.** Once it has run, 0.9.97 and earlier can't open your library. LANcast backs the database up first ("Before an update" in Backup & storage), so going back to 0.9.97 means reinstalling it and restoring that backup with its `restore` command.
