## LANcast 1.0

LANcast is a self-hosted media server. Point it at the folders where your films, shows, music, photos, comics, audiobooks and games already live. It identifies them, gives them real artwork and details, and plays them in its own desktop window, in any browser on your network, or on your phone. It runs on your own computer, needs no account with anyone, and does not phone home.

1.0 is a promise rather than a feature drop. Everything these notes say works has been seen working on a real install. Everything that hasn't been seen working is named below. Installing, updating and the Linux archive were each run start to finish on the published files before this release.

## What it does

- **Films and TV.** Plays almost anything. It plays the file as it is when your device can, re-packages it when only the container is the problem, and converts it with your graphics card when it has to. Seeking and resume work on every path. It finds intros and credits so you can skip them, and has chapters, extras, subtitles (including from OpenSubtitles), collections and a "Continue watching" row.
- **Music.** Albums identified through MusicBrainz, gapless playback, ReplayGain levels, genres, playlists (it reads `.m3u`), and MilkDrop visualizers.
- **Photos, comics and audiobooks.** Photos are grouped by face and place, and both are optional. Comics are read from `.cbz`, `.cb7` and PDF. Audiobooks keep your place and have a sleep timer that can stop at the end of a chapter.
- **Retro games and your PC games.** Plays NES, SNES, Game Boy Advance, Mega Drive, N64 and more in the desktop window, with saves kept on the server and RetroAchievements. Your installed Steam, GOG, EA and Xbox games appear alongside, and launch from there.
- **People.** Accounts for everyone in the house, each with its own libraries and rating limit, plus invite links, passkeys and two-factor sign-in.
- **Friends.** Pair your server with a friend's to see each other's shared libraries and watch together, across the internet, in sync.
- **Your own devices.** Send what's playing from one of your screens to another.
- **Corrections stick.** Fix a title, a poster or a match, and no rescan or refresh will ever undo it.
- **It keeps itself current.** The server checks for, downloads, verifies and installs its own signed updates, when you say so.

## Installing

On **Windows 10 or 11 (x64)**, run **`LANcast-Setup-1.0.0.exe`**. It installs the app and a Windows service that keeps the server running in the background. The first page you see creates your account and offers to download ffmpeg, which converting video needs. [install.md](https://github.com/Conqueror-Mod/LANcast/blob/main/docs/install.md) has the details, including the archive for a manual install.

On **Linux (amd64)**, the server runs as a systemd service, and you use it from a browser. See the Linux section of install.md. A Linux arm64 build is published too, but it is *built, not run*: nobody has tried it on an arm64 machine yet.

Until you set a password, LANcast answers only on the computer it runs on. It opens to your network only once an account protects it.

## Updating from a version before 1.0

- **From 0.11.x:** use the in-app update (Settings, General).
- **From anything older:** run `LANcast-Setup-1.0.0.exe` over the top. It keeps your data folder, your libraries and your watch history.

The database changes are one-way, as they always have been, so take a backup first if you might want to go back. Releases before 1.0 have been archived. Their full story is in [history.md](https://github.com/Conqueror-Mod/LANcast/blob/main/docs/history.md).

## For people writing their own client

The HTTP API is documented in `docs/api.md` and `docs/openapi.json`, and both are tested against the server on every change. **The API's version is still 1, and 1.0 does not change it.** The app's 1.0 and the API's v1 are separate numbers.

## Not in 1.0

- **Live TV** is built and switched off. Its guide and channel handling aren't ready. Everything is kept, and it comes back in a later release.
- **Theater output:** sending HDR to the display and audio passthrough are off by default, and haven't been tested on a full home-theater setup.
- **RetroAchievements hardcore mode** is built and hidden until RetroAchievements approves LANcast.
- **Not built:** Chromecast and AirPlay, offline downloads, ebooks (EPUB), `.cbr` comics, photo bursts, Last.fm and Trakt scrobbling, bandwidth limits, sharing parts of a library, single sign-on (OIDC), theme music, artist images, a gamepad that moves around the app outside games, and a dedicated phone or TV app. On a phone or TV, use the browser.

## Known issues

- **Reported once, not reproduced:** music stalling on a phone, and a sign-in refused at a server's public name. If either happens to you, the server's log now says why. Please send it.
- **Retro games on one phone** stuttered steadily in a Game Boy Advance game. It hasn't been diagnosed.
- **ffmpeg runs with the service's full rights**: LocalSystem on Windows, root on Linux. Narrowing it is planned for 1.1. [security.md](https://github.com/Conqueror-Mod/LANcast/blob/main/docs/security.md) says what that means.
- **Seen only with simulated clients, not real ones:** use behind a real Caddy or nginx reverse proxy, metrics read by a real Prometheus, and webhooks into a real Home Assistant.
- **Not seen working on real files** because none were at hand: Game Boy, Game Boy Color and Master System games, synced song lyrics (`.lrc`), and a film's extras in the desktop window. The tests cover each of them.
- **A friend hosting Watch Together** from behind a home router hasn't been tried. Hosting on a reachable server, with friends joining from other networks, has been.
